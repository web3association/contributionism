# Starting a Contributionist organisation

A new organisation provides an opportunity to establish contribution-linked arrangements before substantial ownership interests and financial commitments accumulate. However, describing an organisation as Contributionist is not enough. Its agreements, records and operating processes must explain how the model works in practice.



## **Begin with a limited, understandable design**

A new organisation does not need to implement every mechanism discussed in this resource. A practical starting arrangement might include:

* A clear purpose.
* An appropriate legal structure.
* Agreed compensation.
* Defined governance eligibility.
* A simple method for recording contributions.
* Explicit limits on future compensation and governance rights.
* A process for selecting and reviewing leadership.
* Protection of existing commitments.

Complex contribution scoring and automated governance can be added later if they provide a demonstrable benefit.



## **Establish the legal foundation**

The founders should identify a legal structure capable of supporting the intended arrangement. Relevant questions include:

* Does the organisation need a separate legal identity?
* Who will hold its assets?
* Who is liable for its obligations?
* Can the intended financial rights be issued?
* Are the rights potentially regulated securities?
* Can workers participate in the proposed governance structure?
* What restrictions apply to distributions?

The organisation may use an existing company, cooperative, trust or another available form. The suitability of each depends on jurisdiction and purpose. The economic description of a contribution right can be translated into legally meaningful terms.



## **Document the founding agreement**

A founding agreement should identify:

* The organisation’s purpose.
* Initial contributors.
* Initial assets and financial commitments.
* Contributions made before formal establishment.
* Compensation already paid.
* Outstanding obligations.
* Any founding leadership arrangements.
* The initial process for admitting new contributors.

Founders should distinguish resources they personally own from resources transferred to the organisation. For example, allowing the organisation to use a founder’s equipment is different from transferring ownership of that equipment.



## **Record pre-launch contributions**

Founding work often occurs before formal systems exist. The organisation should agree on how to recognise any research, product development, recruitment, administrative work, cash expenditure, intellectual property and other accepted contributions.

Retrospective estimates may be necessary, but they should identify their evidence and uncertainty. An unexplained founding allocation can become the basis of long-term unfairness even if it is described as contribution-based.



## **Define the first rights**

For each initial right, specify:

* What contribution supports it.
* Whether it concerns compensation, governance or both.
* Its allocation method.
* Its duration or financial limit.
* Transfer restrictions.
* Treatment on departure.
* Protection against amendment.

Organisation should avoid issuing any broad promises such as “a fair future share” without a method for determining what that means.



## **Set initial financial expectations**

Contributors should understand:

* The operating budget.
* Available cash.
* Expected revenue.
* Financing requirements.
* Compensation commitments.
* The possibility that no surplus will be distributed.
* The conditions under which activity may need to stop.

An internal valuation cannot make an underfunded organisation financially secure. If workers are expected to accept reduced or deferred compensation, the arrangement must be lawful, explicit and genuinely understood.



## **Introduce a basic review cycle**

A new organisation might review its arrangements after its first operating period and at defined milestones. Reviews can consider:

* Whether records are accurate.
* Whether compensation is affordable.
* Whether governance is functioning.
* Whether contribution assessments are useful.
* Whether the original assumptions remain reasonable.

Changes should normally apply prospectively. Existing commitments should not become negotiable merely because the organisation is still experimenting.



## **Minimum operating documents**

A practical initial set of documents includes:

1. Purpose and configuration statement.
2. Governing document or constitution.
3. Contributor agreement.
4. Compensation and distribution policy.
5. Rights register.
6. Leadership mandates.
7. Financial forecast.
8. Complaints and amendment procedures.

The objective is a manageable system that contributors can understand and operate instead of a large collection of documents that no one uses.
