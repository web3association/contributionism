# Core commitments and optional choices

Contributionism addresses two connected organisational problems:

* Rights arising from historical contributions can continue indefinitely.
* New contributions may not create corresponding financial or governance rights.

The framework therefore combines limits on historical rights with consistent rules for recognising new contributions.



## **Core commitment: contribution-linked compensation**

Organisations should establish clear compensation arrangements for accepted labour and capital contributions provided in expectation of a return. Comparable contributions should receive consistent access to the applicable arrangements.

For labour, a recommended default is that comparable workers have access to the organisation’s agreed additional reward system, whether that consists of temporary surplus-sharing, shared benefits or another suitable arrangement. Financial participation should not depend solely on being selected for an ownership grant. This does not require identical compensation. Differences can reflect responsibility, value, risk and other relevant criteria.

Capital may be compensated through a different instrument, such as a defined repayment or contingent financing agreement. Equal treatment does not require treating different contribution relationships as identical.



## **Core commitment: appropriate governance participation**

Governance rights should reflect the decisions involved and the relevant contributors’ responsibilities and interests. An organisation should explain:

* Who qualifies.
* Which decisions they can influence.
* How voting or representation works.
* Which responsibilities are delegated.
* Why particular rights are restricted.

Not every contribution must create every governance right. However, exclusions should not simply reproduce unaccountable control by an established group.



## **Core commitment: limits on historical rights**

A contribution should not automatically create unlimited, perpetual financial participation or authority. Rights should have clearly specified limits, such as:

* A defined surplus-sharing period.
* A defined financial entitlement.
* Continuing active-membership conditions.
* A limited governance window.
* A renewable leadership term.

The type of limit must fit the right. A debt that remains unpaid is not equivalent to an unlimited claim on future surplus. Expiry of future eligibility should not erase an accrued payment obligation.



## **Core commitment: protection of issued rights**

A right must be more than an expression of goodwill. The organisation should respect its agreed:

* Eligibility.
* Calculation method.
* Duration.
* Payment conditions.
* Transfer terms.
* Amendment protections.

Current decision-makers should not be able to remove historical rights arbitrarily or avoid them through self-serving financial decisions.



## **Core commitment: accountable differences**

Different rights or rewards should have an identifiable basis. Organisations may adopt different roles, compensation levels and decision-making powers. They should explain those differences and provide a way to challenge inconsistent treatment. Transparent explanation is necessary, but not sufficient where a restriction is unlawful or substantively abusive.



## **Core commitment: transparent arrangements**

Contributors should be able to understand the rules relevant to their participation before committing significant work or resources. Those rules should include:

* Compensation terms.
* Governance eligibility.
* Duration and expiry.
* Transfer restrictions.
* Amendment procedures.
* Dispute resolution.
* Relevant risks.



## **Optional mechanisms**

Organisations can choose among:

* Equal or contribution-weighted voting.
* Representative governance.
* Individual or team rewards.
* Shared benefits.
* Temporary surplus-sharing.
* Limited-return financing.
* Different contribution valuation methods.
* Different leadership structures.

No single scoring system or technology is required.



## **Remaining specification questions**

The framework could benefit from more precise boundaries and details concerning:

* Whether fixed-pay-only labour arrangements can qualify, and under what conditions.
* Minimum protections for non-voting contributors.
* Permissible renewal of historical rights.
* Treatment of residual assets.
* Maximum or default durations.
* Compliance and verification.
