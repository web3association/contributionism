# Leadership roles and contracts

Leadership can help an organisation coordinate complex work and make timely decisions. Contributionism seeks to make leadership authority explicit, limited and accountable. A leadership contract or role charter can support this objective.



## **Purpose of a leadership arrangement**

The arrangement should explain why the role exists. Possible purposes include:

* Coordinating several teams.
* Executing an agreed strategy.
* Managing specialised risks.
* Making time-sensitive decisions.
* Representing the organisation externally.
* Leading a defined development phase.

An organisation does not need to create leadership positions with broad authority merely because a person has historically been influential.



## **Role holder**

The document should identify the person or body appointed and the process used to select them. It should also state the qualifications or selection criteria relevant to the role. Legal appointment requirements may apply to directors and other formal officers.



## **Scope of authority**

The role should identify:

* Decisions the leader may make independently.
* Decisions requiring consultation.
* Decisions requiring approval.
* Decisions reserved to another body.
* Financial limits.
* Authority to delegate further.

Specific authority is easier to evaluate than a general policy to “run the organisation”.



## **Duration**

A defined term creates a natural point for review. However, fixed terms do not prevent the same person from serving repeatedly if contributors continue to support them. Continuity can be beneficial. The important distinction is between renewable authority and an automatic permanent entitlement.

A leadership term ending may also have employment consequences that require separate treatment under applicable law.



## **Responsibilities and standards**

The arrangement can specify:

* Expected outcomes.
* Reporting frequency.
* Conduct requirements.
* Conflict-of-interest obligations.
* Confidentiality.
* Safety and compliance responsibilities.

Performance should not be assessed solely through short-term financial results. A leader may improve immediate profit while weakening capability, trust or resilience.



## **Compensation**

Leadership compensation should be documented separately from authority. A person’s position does not automatically establish that their contribution is more valuable than every other contribution. An organisation may reward exceptional non-managerial work more highly than a leadership role. Any incentive package should be examined for the behaviour it encourages.



## **Review and renewal**

The review process should consider:

* Performance against the mandate.
* Quality of decisions.
* Contributor feedback.
* Compliance.
* Whether the role remains necessary.
* Whether its scope should change.

Renewal should be a real decision rather than an automatic formality.



## **Early removal**

The organisation should define grounds and procedures for early removal. These may include:

* Serious misconduct.
* Material breach of authority.
* Loss of confidence.
* Persistent failure to perform.
* Elimination of the role.

Removal processes should respect applicable law and provide appropriate procedural fairness. The organisation may need temporary arrangements to preserve continuity while a dispute is resolved.



## **Overruling decisions**

A constitution may allow certain leadership decisions to be reviewed or vetoed. Such powers should be carefully scoped. If every routine decision can be immediately reopened by a large group, the role may become ineffective. If no decision can be challenged, accountability may be inadequate.



## **Transparency**

People should be able to understand the authority exercised over them. The organisation should disclose the role’s scope and accountability arrangements to relevant contributors, while protecting personal information and legitimate confidential terms. Public disclosure of every contractual detail is not always necessary.



## **Emergent leadership**

Not every leadership role must be formally created in advance. People may naturally lead where they have relevant expertise or initiative. The important questions are whether:

* Others can challenge the arrangement.
* Responsibilities remain clear.
* Informal influence becomes hidden, unaccountable control.
* The organisation should formalise the role as it grows.
